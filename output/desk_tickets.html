    Verified offer: 8 units × $52.00 = $416.00 if accepted. Discount 10.34%; gross profit $30.00/unit and $240.00 total; gross margin 57.69%. The $176.00 cost basis is already-held inventory, not a new payment. New purchase cost for the full shortfall would be $264.00.

    The initial model decision incorrectly called $52 exactly 10% off $58 and blurred existing inventory cost with new spending. A deterministic MCP quote tool and a separate real review run corrected those claims. Keeping both versions in the audit makes this a measured correction rather than an unexplained edit. This is also a limit of relying on language-model arithmetic without tool checks.

    Where one agent with tools would be simpler

    Rent is essentially a lease lookup, cash check, approval request and transactional update. One agent with those tools could do it with fewer handoffs while retaining the exact same human payment gate. The tee request also needs only a stock check, a linked-invoice check and a draft; separating those into roles makes responsibilities visible, but repeats context and reads. The hoodie case benefits more from distinct stock and financial reviews, although one well-instructed agent could still compute both. Specialist names do not provide safety by themselves: amount validation, cash checks and route isolation do.

    Three new problems these tools can address

    1. A customer asks for a currently stocked large navy hoodie: Inventory can check SKU and size, Accounting can check the listed price, and Customer Service can draft an accurate availability response. The existing tools support the desk response, not a completed sale.
    2. A landlord asks whether a recorded rent obligation was paid: Facilities can inspect the lease and Accounting can verify payment history, with Boss recording a documented answer.
    3. A club requests a different quantity or discount on a stocked product: Inventory can establish capacity, Accounting can compute cost and gross margin, and Boss can approve a proposed price exception for a draft. A new ticket would currently need to be inserted by a manager because this app has no intake tool.

    Three problems needing more tools or agents

    1. A customer wants to pay for the hoodie offer. Add checkout, payment collection, sales invoices and inventory reservation/fulfillment tools, plus a sales or fulfillment role. This simulation deliberately models no incoming revenue.
    2. A supplier claims a shipment was delivered, but goods are missing. Add purchase-order line items, supplier confirmations, shipment tracking and receiving/count tools, with a receiving or logistics role. Paying a reprint bill cannot establish what physically arrived.
    3. A customer asks for a damaged-item refund. Add order lookup, return authorization, refund execution and stock-condition records, with a returns role and a separate human refund approval path. Current customer drafts and generic bill payments are insufficient.

    Limits of this evaluation

    These are three tickets in a small fixed database, not a broad accuracy benchmark. The evidence tests routing, state transitions and cash control. It does not test actual supplier service, delivery, customer satisfaction, revenue or demand forecasting. This reflection is based on the recorded execution, including failed attempts and subsequent reviews.
